# Distillate: TEI P5 4.12.0 Guidelines chapter Names, Dates, People, and Places

This distillate reports what the English prose of the Guidelines chapter Names, Dates, People, and Places states at the pinned TEI P5 4.12.0 release commit about the purpose of the namesdates module, the naming elements and their relation to the core elements, the linking of a name to its referent and to its nym, the person, organization and place records, and the traits, states, events and relations that describe those entities.

## Core statements

- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines describe the module of this chapter as one for encoding names and other phrases descriptive of persons, places or organizations in more detail than the Core module elements allow, noting that those core elements let an encoder mark a text segment as a proper noun or a "referring string" and specify the kind of object named or referred to only through a value of the `type` attribute, whereas the elements of this module supply a detailed sub-structure for such referring strings and distinguish explicitly between names of persons, places and organizations. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b2]] ^s1
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the module also provides elements to represent information about the person, place or organization to which a given name is understood to refer and to represent the name itself independently of its application, so that where the core module can only represent that a piece of text is a "name", this module can further represent a "personal name", the "person" being named and the "canonical name" being used, with biographical, historical or geographical data systems such as gazetteers and biographical databases integrated with encoded texts as the main intended applications. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b3]] ^s2
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `att.naming` is a subclass of `att.canonical`, from which it inherits `key` and `ref` as two different ways of associating any sort of name with its referent, that `att.naming` adds a simple `role` attribute for minimal information about the person named such as occupation or status and a `nymRef` attribute for associating the name itself with a base or canonical form, that `ref` should be used wherever it is possible to supply a direct link such as a URI to the location of canonical information about the referent, which requires that a `person` element with that identifier exist somewhere, possibly in another document, and that more than one URI may be supplied where the name refers to more than one person. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b8]] ^s3
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines provide the `key` attribute for cases where no direct link is required, either because resolution of the reference is carried out by some local convention, as when a project maintains its own local database whose entries are accessed through a system-specific identifier constructed from the `key` value, or because the encoder judges that no resolution is necessary, as with well-established codifications such as country or airport codes. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b9]] ^s4
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that interchange is improved by the use of tag URIs in `ref` instead of `key`, and point to another section of the Guidelines for the explanation. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b10]] ^s5
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines give `nymRef` a more specialized use, for cases where the name itself is of interest rather than the person, place or organization being named. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b11]] ^s6
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the core `rs` and `name` elements can distinguish names in a text but are insufficiently powerful to mark their internal components or structure, that distinguishing a family name, a forename and an honorary title matters for nominal record linkage or even for an alphabetically sorted list of personal names, and that the elements `persName`, `surname`, `forename`, `roleName`, `addName`, `nameLink` and `genName` are provided for these and related purposes. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b26]] ^s7
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `persName` may be used in preference to the general `name` element whether or not the components of the personal name are also marked, that `persName` is synonymous with `name type="person"` except that its own `type` attribute allows further subcategorization of the personal name itself, for example as a `married`, `birth`, `pen`, `pseudo` or `religious` name, and that encodings of one name with `rs`, with `name` inside `rs`, with `name` and with `persName`, each carrying the same `ref` value, are consequently equivalent. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b28]] ^s8
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the `type` attribute on `forename` and `surname` supplies culture- or project-specific detail about a name component, that its values are not constrained and may be chosen to suit the encoding needs of the project, and that they may distinguish kinds of forename or surname as well as indicate the function a component fills within the whole name, as in the example of a toponymic surname whose `ref` points to the place name from which it derives. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b31]] ^s9
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines offer alternative encodings for a patronymic, which may be treated as a forename, tagged as a `surname` when an individual who would otherwise have no surname uses it as such in a culture that requires surnames, marked with `addName` as a distinct kind of name that is neither forename nor surname, or distinguished from other forenames by a `type` value on `forename`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b35]] ^s10
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines use `addName` and `roleName` for all name components other than those already listed in the section, distinguish the two in that `roleName` encloses an associated component such as an aristocratic or official title that exists in some sense independently of its bearer, admit that this distinction is not always clear, and state that the role a person has in a given context, such as witness or defendant in a legal document, should not be encoded with `roleName`, because that element is intended for roles that function as part of a person's name, while information about a person's roles and occupations belongs within the `person` element. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b39]] ^s11
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the mechanisms for marking personal name components, although highly flexible, will not cater for every personal name nor for every processing need, and recommend feature structures as the most appropriate mechanism where the internal structure of personal names is highly complex or where name components are particularly ambiguous. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b42]] ^s12
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines use the term "organization" for any named collection of people regarded as a single unit, including institutions and businesses as well as racial or ethnic groupings or political factions regarded as forming a single agency, and observe that giving a loosely defined group of individuals a name often serves a political or social agenda, so that an analysis of such phrases may matter to the social historian even where the objective existence of an organization in this sense is harder to demonstrate than that of a named person. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b45]] ^s13
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that organizational names, like names of persons or places, can be marked up as referring strings with `rs` or as proper names with `name`, that `orgName` is provided where organizational names are to be distinguished more explicitly, and that `orgName` belongs to the same attribute classes as `persName`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b46]] ^s14
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines describe an `orgName` encoding as equivalent to but more specific than the same text encoded with `rs` or `name`, state that `orgName`, like those elements, has a `key` attribute for assigning an external identifier such as a database key to the organization name and a `ref` attribute for pointing directly to an `org` element holding information about the organization itself, and state that its `type` attribute should characterize the name rather than the organization, for example as an acronym, a phrase or a composite of other kinds of name. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b47]] ^s15
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that place names, like other proper nouns or noun phrases used as names, can simply be marked up with `rs` or with `name`, while for cartographers and historical geographers the component parts of a place name carry important information about the relation between the name and some spot in space and time and provide evidence for historical linguistics. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b52]] ^s16
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines distinguish three ways of referring to places, a place name represented with `placeName` that may consist of one or more names of hierarchically organized geo-political or administrative units, a place named in terms of geographical features such as mountains or rivers represented with `geogName`, and an expression of spatial or other relationships between other named places, which is itself regarded as a kind of named place. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b53]] ^s17
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `placeName`, like `persName`, may be regarded simply as an abbreviation for `name type="place"` or `rs type="place"`, so that the two versions of their example are equivalent, and add in a footnote that strictly a value such as `figurative` should be given to the periphrastic place names in the `placeName` version to preserve the distinction that the choice of `rs` rather than `name` had indicated. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b55]] ^s18
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the internal structure of place names is, like that of personal names, complex and subject to enormous variation across time and cultures, that the recommendations of the section should be adequate for a majority of users and applications and may be extended with new elements in the existing classes through the mechanisms of another chapter, that the elements referenced at that point may be of use when the focus is on the name components themselves, and that the meaning structure may alternatively be represented with feature structures. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b67]] ^s19
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines propose a flexible strategy in which encoders may choose for themselves the approach appropriate to their needs, giving as their example that one interested in converting existing DNB-type records and wanting to preserve the text as is could let the `person` element simply contain the text of an article placed within `p` elements, possibly using elements such as `name` or `date` to mark up features of that text, while for a more structured entry one would extract the data and place information contained in the text and encode it directly using the more specific elements described in that section. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b82]] ^s20
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that information about people, places, organizations and events essentially comprises statements or assertions about "traits", characteristics which by and large do not change over time, about "states", characteristics which hold true only at a specific time, about "events", incidents which may lead to a change of state or less frequently of trait, and about external resources where other information on the subject can be found. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b84]] ^s21
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines describe characteristics or traits as typically independent of an individual's volition or action and as either physical or cultural, state that the distinction is not entirely straightforward because gender and sex can be regarded as culturally determined with physical manifestations, state that the division of humanity into "races" formalized in the nineteenth century is now recognized as a colonialist social construct, and ask that where such a construct defined individuals' identities beyond their volition the markup and documentation reflect this as a culturally determined characteristic. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b85]] ^s22
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines describe states, with marital status, place of residence and position or occupation as examples, as having a definite duration with a beginning and an end and as typically a consequence of the individual's own action or that of others. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b86]] ^s23
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines describe changes in state as life-changing or identity-changing events such as birth, marriage or appointment to office, normally associated with a specific date or a fairly narrow date range, and conclude that because any statement about these aspects of a person's life rests on some source, possibly several and possibly contradictory, each such statement needs to be documentable, put into a time frame and relatable to other statements. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b87]] ^s24
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that when developing a prosopography record of a named entity it is a common practice to refer explicitly to other resources, naming the Library of Congress Name Authority File, the Virtual International Authority File, a gazetteer of places like Pleiades and a printed book as examples, and follow that statement with a `specList` naming `idno` with its `type` attribute. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b89]] ^s25
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines group information about a person, as distinct from references to a person, for example by name, within a `person` element, state that information about a group of people regarded as a single entity, for example the audience of a performance, may be encoded using the `personGrp` element, and note that information about a group of people with a distinct identity, for example a named theatrical troupe, should be recorded using the `org` element. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b92]] ^s26
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that these person descriptions may appear only within a `listPerson` element, which groups them together and optionally also describes relationships amongst the people listed, and name `listPerson` and `listRelation` for this purpose. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b93]] ^s27
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the `type` attribute may be used to distinguish lists of people of different kinds where this is considered convenient, and illustrate this with a `listPerson` of type `fictional` and a `listPerson` of type `real` inside `standOff`, whose `person` entries carry the same personal names in both lists while the notes of the first describe roles across numbered seasons of works named with `title` and the notes of the second describe offices and occupations with dates. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b95]] ^s28
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the `age` attribute of `person` records the age bracket into which a person falls for the purposes of some analysis rather than an age in years or other temporal units, that the brackets useful to projects are likely to be varied and idiosyncratic so that the Guidelines make no particular recommendation as to possible values, and that projects are recommended to define the values they use in their own customization file. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b100]] ^s29
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the `person` element may contain many sub-elements, each specifying a different property of the person being described, that these elements are for convenience grouped into three classes, one for traits, one for states and one for events, and that each class may contain specific elements for common types of biographical information and contains a generic element for other, user-defined, types of information. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b101]] ^s30
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines instruct that where none of the specialized elements is appropriate a choice must be made between the generic `trait` and `state`, that `state` serves characteristics generally perceived as transient and `trait` those generally considered unchanging, that traits are typically but not necessarily independent of the volition or action of the holder, and that `state` is to be used where the distinction between state and trait is not considered relevant or useful. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b115]] ^s31
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `persName` is repeatable within `person` with `xml:lang` for the language of the name and `type` for the kind of name, contrast this with the library-world method of accepting the form found in an authority file such as that of the Library of Congress as the "base" or "neutral" form, note that people are often reluctant to accept an overtly foreign form of the name of a local saint or hero as neutral, and conclude that within `person` any number of variant forms can be given with no prioritization and hence less likelihood of offence, as in the example of a scholar known by Icelandic, Danish and Latin forms of his name. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b116]] ^s32
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines allow the generic `state` or `trait` element to be used in preference to the more specific elements, and present a `state` of type `nationality` with `label` and `desc`, a `nationality` element with the same text and dating attribute, and an empty `nationality` element carrying only a `key` and the dating attribute as encodings of the same information. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b119]] ^s33
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines distinguish events in a person's history from characteristics of the individual, since events often cause an individual to gain characteristics or to enter a new state, state that most such events, for example marriage, appointment, promotion or a journey, may be recorded with the generic `event` element grouped by `listEvent`, and state that `event` has a content model similar to that of `state` and `trait` with the chief difference that it can include a `placeName` for the place where the event occurred. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b122]] ^s34
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines encode the wedding of their example as an `event` embedded within the `person` element of one spouse while stating that it could equally well have been embedded within the `person` element of the other spouse or encoded independently of either, note that the `ref` attributes on the `name` elements of the description point either to an external source or to a `person` element holding further information about the person named, and add that a `relation` element may then link the persons in a more meaningful way. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b124]] ^s35
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that all these elements, specific and generic, are members of `att.datable` and can therefore be limited in terms of time, and illustrate this with a `person` holding one `persName` with `notAfter` and another with `notBefore` for a person who changed name in a given year. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b125]] ^s36
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the generic elements are members of `att.global.responsibility` and `att.editLike`, which make available `cert` for the degree of certainty, `resp` for the agency responsible, `evidence` for the nature of the evidence used and `source` as a pointer to the resource from which the information derives, so that in the case of multiple and conflicting sources more than one view of what happened can be provided, as in the example of two `event` elements of type `birth` with different places, dates, responsible agents and certainty values. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b126]] ^s37
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that when the module defined by this chapter is included in a schema, the two elements `listRelation` and `relation` may be used to document relationships amongst the persons, places or organizations identified, select `name`, `active`, `mutual` and `passive` among the attributes of `relation` for documentation, state that both elements are members of `att.typed` from which they inherit `type` and `subtype`, and state that the value specified for either of those attributes on a `listRelation` element is implicitly applicable to all of its child `relation` elements unless overridden. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b128]] ^s38
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines define a "relationship" as any kind of describable link between specified participants, where a participant might be a person, a place or an organization, distinguish a "mutual" relationship in which all participants engage on an equal footing from one in which the participants are not identical with respect to their role, state that for non-mutual relationships only two kinds of role are currently supported and that these are named "active" and "passive" to reflect the fact that non-mutual relations are "directed" in the sense that they are most readily described by a transitive verb or a verb phrase of the form "is X of" or "is X to", with the subject of the verb classed as active and its object as passive, and note that these relationships can be inverted, so that parents are passive and children active in the relationship "is child of". [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b129]] ^s39
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `org` and `listOrg` store data about an organization such as its preferred name, its locations or key persons within it, in a way analogous to `place` and `person`, meaning a unique wrapper for information about an entity distinct from references to that entity, which are typically encoded with a naming element such as `name type="org"` or `orgName`, so that the content of a naming element represents the way an organization is named in a given context while the content of `org` represents the information known to the encoder about that organization, gathered in a single place and independent of its textual realization. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b133]] ^s40
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that an organization is not the same thing as a list or group of people because it has an identity of its own, that this identity may be expressed solely in the existence of a name but is likely to consist in the combination of that name with a number of events, traits or states considered to apply to the organization itself rather than to any of its members, and say of their sports team example that the properties which categorize it, such as the kind of sport played, are probably best dealt with by means of the `type` attribute while it is the name of the sports team alone which identifies it. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b134]] ^s41
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines distinguish the encoding of names for places from the encoding of other data about places in the same way as they distinguish names for people from other data about people, and present the place elements as a structured record of data about any place that might be named or referenced within a text, useful for normalizing or standardizing references to particular places, as the raw material for a gazetteer or similar reference document, or in conjunction with a geographical information system. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b141]] ^s42
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines take the modern city of Lyon and the Roman Lugdunum, which are not physically co-extensive but overlap significantly, as an example of what an encoder may wish to regard as the same place while supplying both names with an indication of the time period during which each was current. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b144]] ^s43
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines give as an example of an imaginary place a `place` with `type="imaginary"` whose `placeName` is Atlantis and whose `location` consists of an `offset` and a further `placeName`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b153]] ^s44
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `location` may identify a place to a greater or lesser degree of precision by a name, a set of names or a set of coordinates, that more than one `geo` within one `location` is interpreted as the same place in the universe referred to through different systems, and that where there is a lack of consensus about the location of a place, such as Camelot, more than one `location` should be used, each with its own `geo`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b156]] ^s45
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state of their Lithuania example that it does not imply that Vilnius and Kaunas are the only places constituting Lithuania, only that they are within it, and that a separate `place` element may indicate that it is a part of Lithuania by supplying a `relation` element. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b165]] ^s46
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `country`, `region` and `settlement` specialize the generic `placeName` element rather than the `place` element, so that kinds of place can be distinguished only by means of the `type` attribute. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b168]] ^s47
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines distinguish the use of multiple `place` elements from the possibly simpler case in which a number of places with some property in common are grouped together for convenience, for example in a gazetteer, and provide `listPlace` as a means of grouping places together where there is no implication that the grouped elements constitute a distinct place. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b169]] ^s48
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the kinds of information worth recording for a place beyond its name and location are likely to be very project-specific, that they therefore make no claim to comprehensiveness for places any more than for persons, that the generic `state`, `trait` and `event` elements customized through their `type` attribute should be used instead, and that these are complemented by the predefined elements `population`, `climate` and `terrain`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b171]] ^s49
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that `state`, `trait` and other elements of the same class can be nested hierarchically with `type` values understood as cumulatively inherited, that responsibility is not an additive property so that an element either states it explicitly or inherits it from its nearest ancestor, and that dating differs again in that a child element may specify a date more precisely than its parent. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b175]] ^s50
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines describe a "stand-off" style of representation whose advantage is that a place may be represented as a "part of" more than one other place, give as their example that Réunion is part of France as well as part of the Mascarenes, and model that dual allegiance by means of a `relation` element whose `name` is `partOf` and which carries one identifier in `active` and two in `passive`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b179]] ^s51
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines define an event as a change of state, whether real, planned, fictional or purported, about which more information is known, allow `eventName` to encode the proper name of an event if known, and allow `head` or `label` within `event` instead of or in addition to `eventName` to provide a human-readable heading or an editorially supplied label. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b182]] ^s52
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines do not prescribe what encoders count as events, nor their granularity, their nesting or the amount of other information recorded in relation to each event, while observing that most events can be identified by their time, their location or the agents involved, whether organizations, persons or objects. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b183]] ^s53
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that an `event` element may record information about a place, person or organization related to the described event and may therefore appear as content of `place`, `person` or `org`, and that events may also be described independently of any of these, which serves both the encoding of texts that describe named events and applications such as chronologies or lists of significant events. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b184]] ^s54
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines summarize the resolution of a name or referring string by considering the object it refers to, effected through `key` or `ref` on any member of `att.naming`, and then treat names as objects in their own right irrespective of the objects they are attached to, notably in onomastic studies, using the term "nym" for the canonical or normalized form of a name regarded in this way and providing `listNym` and `nym` to encode it. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b205]] ^s55
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that any element which is a member of `att.naming` may use the attribute `nymRef` to indicate the nym with which it corresponds, illustrate this with a `nym` for the name "Antony" and an occurrence in running text encoded as a `forename` reading "Tony" whose `nymRef` points to that `nym`, and note that this association has nothing to do with any individual who might use the name. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b206]] ^s56
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that a schema using `nym` or `listNym` must include the `dictionaries` module as well as the `namesdates` module, which makes many elements beyond `form` available, such as `etym` for an etymological decomposition of a name. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b209]] ^s57
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the Guidelines state that the `nym` element may also combine a number of other `nym` elements together where it is intended to show that they are all regarded as variations on the same root, and represent the different forms of the name John as a hierarchic structure whose `form` elements carry the `xml:lang` values `la`, `en`, `ru` and `fr`, except for two forms held by `nym` elements nested inside the one that holds the English form, which carry no language attribute. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b212]] ^s58
- In TEI P5 4.12.0, the chapter declares the module `namesdates` with the formal public identifier "Names, dates, persons and places" and the English description "Names and dates". [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b243]] ^s59

## Terms

- **referring string**: a text segment marked as referring to a person, place or organization, encoded with `rs` where a proper name is encoded with `name`. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b46]]
- **organization**: any named collection of people regarded as a single unit. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b45]]
- **trait**: a characteristic which by and large does not change over time. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b84]]
- **state**: a characteristic which holds true only at a specific time. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b84]]
- **event**: a change of state, whether real, planned, fictional or purported, about which more information is known. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b182]]
- **relationship**: any kind of describable link between specified participants, each of which may be a person, a place or an organization. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b129]]
- **active and passive**: the two roles supported for non-mutual, directed relationships, the active participant being the subject of the verb that describes the relationship and the passive participant its object. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b129]]
- **nym**: the canonical or normalized form of a name regarded as an object in its own right, irrespective of the objects to which the name is attached. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b205]]
- **persona**: a set of characteristics associated with one specific individual, as distinct from a role, which is a set of characteristics that many different people can assume. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b108]]
- **relative place name**: a specification of a place in terms of its relationship to another place, containing a referent place name, a word or phrase for the position relative to the referent and possibly a distance. [[10_markdown/documents/tei-p5-guidelines-nd-4.12.0#^b65]]

## Open questions

- The chapter presents `persName` as synonymous with `name type="person"` and `placeName` as an abbreviation for `name type="place"` or `rs type="place"`, while its footnote says that only an added `type` value preserves the distinction between `rs` and `name`. Does the chapter regard the distinction between a referring string and a proper name as recoverable from `persName` or `placeName` at all?
- The chapter says `ref` should be used wherever a direct link can be supplied, offers `key` for local conventions and well-established codes, and says interchange improves with tag URIs in `ref`, while delegating the reasoning to another section. Which rule applies when both a local key and a URI are available for the same referent?
- The chapter states that person descriptions may appear only within `listPerson`, while several of its own examples place `person` elements directly beside `relation` or `forename` elements. Is the constraint schematic or a statement of recommended practice?
- The chapter says an encoder "may wish to regard" a modern city and its overlapping Roman predecessor as the same place. Does the chapter leave the identity of a `place` record to the encoder's decision without further criteria?
- The chapter defines events and objects as possibly fictional or purported and shows fictional persons only through a `listPerson` of type `fictional`. Does it intend `person` records to admit fictional individuals in general?
- Non-mutual relationships "can be inverted" by naming the converse relation. Does the chapter intend one direction to be recorded per relationship, or both?
- A child element within a nested trait may date more precisely than its parent. What does the chapter intend where a child's dates fall outside the parent's range?
- The chapter uses "canonical name" for what the module represents alongside the personal name and the person, and "nym" for the canonical or normalized form of a name. Are these the same notion?

## Appraisal

This chapter is the Guidelines' own prose account of the namesdates module at the pinned release, so it establishes the stated intent, terminology, recommendations and admitted limits of the naming, record, relation and nym mechanisms, including the equivalences the chapter itself asserts between `rs`, `name` and the specialized naming elements. It cannot establish the formal content models, class memberships or attribute datatypes, which live in the element and class specifications, nor the reasoning it delegates by cross-reference to other chapters, and its statements about equivalence and identity are the chapter's own positions rather than observed encoding practice.
